# Benefits and cautions — source and scope brief

Checked **2026-10-07** for topic `M2-T05` in Joga Yoga's public anatomy outline. This supports an introductory distinction, not a contraindications list, clinical advice or the cohort's own safety protocol.

- [NIH NCCIH, Yoga: Effectiveness and Safety](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/yoga-effectiveness-and-safety) summarizes research on possible benefits and notes that styles, study populations and programs differ. It says injuries can occur, that people with health conditions may need modifications, and that individual needs should be discussed with health providers and instructors.
- [Yoga Alliance RYS 200 standards](https://yogaalliance.org/Standards-for-RYS-Credentials) include safe movement, physical limitations, contraindications and adaptations in the Anatomy & Physiology/biomechanics category. This is an interim comparison framework, not the friend's cohort syllabus.
- [Yoga Alliance Scope of Practice](https://yogaalliance.org/policies-priorities-progress/scope-of-practice/) says its members must not provide advice or services outside their current licensing. The lesson presents this as a modern professional boundary, not a school-specific rule.

## Editorial choice

Two visual views: **Evidence** asks the learner to avoid turning a possible benefit into a guarantee; **Person** asks them to recognize that a health condition changes the safety question. The art is an abstract balance of questions and is not a pose diagram. Two scored checks test the distinction; a third prompt invites a question for a trainer without scoring the person's health judgment.

The lesson will not list which poses are safe for named conditions, advise a person to stop treatment, or suggest that a trainee can assess a contraindication independently. A qualified anatomy/yoga educator still needs to review any practical application before wider release.

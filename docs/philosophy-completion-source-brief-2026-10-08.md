## Haṭha Pradīpikā · `M4-T02`

- [Yoga Alliance RYS 200 standards](https://yogaalliance.org/Standards-for-RYS-Credentials), Yoga Humanities p.17, give the Haṭha Yoga Pradīpikā as an example of a major text a school may cover, while leaving exact treatment to the school.
- The [Centre for Yoga Studies translation of chapter 1](https://yogastudies.org/text-section/hatha-pradipika/hp-chapter-1/) identifies and translates verse 1 (Haṭha knowledge pictured as a ladder toward Rāja Yoga) and verse 17 (āsana introduced first among the text's Haṭha limbs). The learner lesson paraphrases only these two points and identifies the passage numbers. It does not reproduce a full translation, teach practices, or promote the text's historical health claims as modern evidence.
- The original ladder/book SVG is a memory aid, not a manuscript image or historical reconstruction. One choice question checks the verse 1 image; an ungraded question returns interpretation to the trainer.

- The [same RYS 200 standards](https://yogaalliance.org/Standards-for-RYS-Credentials) name the Bhagavad Gītā as a possible major text and ask schools how they integrate it, rather than specifying passages or an interpretation.

An [IIT Kanpur Gītā comparison page](https://www.gitasupersite.iitk.ac.in/srimad?field_chapter_value=2&field_nsutra_value=47&language=dv&choose=1&etgb=1) surfaced a translation of 2.47, but the page could not be fully inspected in this session. The public course outline supplies no cohort passage or translation. Seva also needs tradition-specific framing.

This preserves a useful route to class discussion without pretending that one short definition represents every tradition. **Cost if wrong:** less standalone philosophy content now and greater reliance on the trainer; a later course-exact edition can replace the prompts with source-checked, attributed lessons.
